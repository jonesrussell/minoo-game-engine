# Ford Frenzy campaign outline

**Status:** fully outlined creative draft awaiting owner review.  
**Issue:** [#91](https://github.com/jonesrussell/minoo-game-engine/issues/91)  
**Production:** jr42 productions  
**Research:** [Ford Frenzy historical ledger](../research/ford-frenzy-history.md), delivered in #90.

This six chapter outline fixes the playable shape while leaving release approval, rights, final copy, and art selection with the owner. Public history remains fixed. The player is a fictional reporter assembling attributed coverage, never the source of the original video, a police action, a vote, or an election result.

## Logline and cast

At The Ledger Gazette, junior reporter Alex Chen follows a public record through deadlines, denials, institutional responses, and an election. A small newsroom argues about what the city deserves on the record. The story is a humane satire of deadline panic and careful sourcing.

Alex Chen is the player journalist, moving from clip collector to trusted beat writer. Elliot Vance is the city editor, learning to value structure over heat. Priya D'Souza is the photographer, defending caption ethics. Tomás Reyes is the Harbourview Community Desk liaison, reminding Alex that neighborhoods contain more than one mood. Nadia Okafor is copy chief and standards gate. Sasha Bell is the intern, learning sourcing hygiene alongside the player. All dialogue for this cast is original fiction. Real public figures appear only in attributed summaries of dated public events.

## Chapter beats and branch copy

Chapter 1 introduces the search loop and the rule that heat is not a source. Sample branch copy: BR-CH1-A, “File the public account before the desk cools.” BR-CH1-B, “Leave the unknown sentence visible until it can be answered.”

Chapter 2 treats the 24 May denial as an attributed public response, then moves to a fictional 8 June community follow-up. Sample copy: BR-CH2-A, “The sidebar starts with library hours, transit, and who gets heard.” BR-CH2-B, “The sidebar starts with the records gap and the question it leaves open.”

Chapter 3 stages the 31 October announcement without giving the player access to a video. Sample copy: BR-CH3-A, “Keep the chronology deep enough that every date can be checked.” BR-CH3-B, “Put the public claim in plain language before the next alert.”

Chapter 4 covers the 5 November admission, the 13 November request for leave, and the 18 November changed responsibilities. Sample copy: BR-CH4-A, “Write the alert in the voice of a newsroom that is still checking.” BR-CH4-B, “Lead with the procedure and name what the decision does.” This choice is shown in the next chapter's S13 assignment sidebar, never in S11.

Chapter 5 follows the 1 May leave reporting, a fictional 8 May service follow-up, and the 30 June return. Sample copy: BR-CH5-A, “The service desk stays visible while the headline moves.” BR-CH5-B, “Explain who carries which responsibility while the office changes.”

Chapter 6 covers the 12 September candidate change, preliminary 27 October results, and 30 October certification. Sample copy: BR-CH6-A, “Close on Ward 2 representation and the people who live with it.” BR-CH6-B, “Close on the citywide transition and the record that confirms it.”

At the start of each next chapter, the chosen prior branch appears as a sidebar line. For example, BR-CH4-A or BR-CH4-B appears in S13's leave-announcement sidebar. No branch blocks the next chapter. Branches change voice, sidebar emphasis, and Alex's fictional career reflection only. They never change historical outcomes.

## Clue graph and review rules

The progression is a simple chain. S01 awards K01, then S02 requires K01 and awards K02, and so on through S18 and K18. The only branch decision occurs after S03, S06, S09, S12, S15, and S18. In notation:

`S01 -> K01 -> S02 -> K02 -> S03 -> K03 -> BR-CH1 -> S04 -> K04 -> S05 -> K05 -> S06 -> K06 -> BR-CH2 -> S07 -> K07 -> S08 -> K08 -> S09 -> K09 -> BR-CH3 -> S10 -> K10 -> S11 -> K11 -> S12 -> K12 -> BR-CH4 -> S13 -> K13 -> S14 -> K14 -> S15 -> K15 -> BR-CH5 -> S16 -> K16 -> S17 -> K17 -> S18 -> K18 -> BR-CH6`

Each editorial pass checks all six finds and the scene's one review requirement. A failed pass returns to that scene's review step. There are no penalties, trust scores, hints penalties, or simulated relationship mechanics. Characters develop through original dialogue and the consequences of what Alex chooses to print.

## Deterministic endings

The six branch choices are six binary choices, BR-CH1-A/B through BR-CH6-A/B. There are no trust bands, relationship scores, hidden hints, or retry conditions. Every one of the 64 combinations reaches one of two supportive fictional outcomes:

- **END-A, Record of the city:** if at least three of these choices are selected: CH1-B, CH2-B, CH3-A, CH4-B, CH5-B, CH6-B. Sample copy: “The record holds because every sentence shows its work.” Alex receives an offer to stay on the city beat.
- **END-B, People behind headlines:** otherwise. Sample copy: “The record is a beginning, and the people in it are still here.” Alex receives a Harbourview community column assignment.

Both endings show the same certified history. Each ending has two numbered shots:

1. A close shot of Alex's marked notebook beside Nadia's final copy note, followed by the ending's sample line.
2. A wide newsroom shot with Elliot, Priya, Tomás, Nadia, and Sasha resolving their fictional work relationships as the next assignment lands.

No branch can prevent John Tory being named mayor or Rob Ford being named winner of Ward 2 Etobicoke North in S18. The player's six choices affect only voice, sidebar emphasis, and fictional career reflection.

## Tone, boundaries, and candidate art

Use attribution labels for public record, attributed allegation, and original fiction. Do not depict the original video, a secret dealer, a diagnosis, private health details, invented real-person dialogue, police action caused by the player, or player-controlled Council votes and elections. Satire targets publicity, bureaucracy, and deadline chaos, while Toronto neighborhoods remain lived-in communities.

Art references are candidates pending owner selection. ff-011, ff-073, and ff-075 are logo and civic palette references, not desk textures. ff-044 is a City Hall hallway reference, not a generic newsroom. ff-079 and ff-080 are candidate non-combat silhouettes.

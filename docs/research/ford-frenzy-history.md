# Ford Frenzy: historical ledger

Research checkpoint: 7 September 2026. Issue #90. This ledger supports a proposed
six-chapter campaign, May 2013 to October 2014, with a December 2014 closing card.
2010 establishes background. It is a selected chronology, not every controversy
in the mayoral term. The game, fictional cast and staging remain draft.

## How to read and use this record

- H identifiers are historical claims. K identifiers in storyboards are fictional
  notebook products, not new historical evidence.
- **Fact** means the narrow recorded public event. **Attribution** means what a
  speaker or publication said, without promoting everything said to proven fact.
- Every source below was retrieved or its indexed text inspected on the research
  date. A blocked original is explicitly marked. Search crawl timestamps and 2017
  file migration paths are not original publication dates.
- Source references permit verification, not reproduction. Production clearance
  for proposed archival text, photos, recordings, logos and facsimiles is **pending
  assessment**; this is an internal status, not a legal finding about each source.
  Use short original paraphrases and original staging. Human editorial review is
  **pending** for every claim and portrayal, independently of research completion.
- Store event date, first usable public date, source-document date and current
  access date separately. For a retrospective source, the historical event may
  have been public earlier; the source artifact itself may not appear earlier.

## Creative treatment and recovered concept

The playable reporter, editor, photographer, community contact and newsroom are
original fictional characters. Their assignments, conversations, schedules, bills,
reader letters and interpersonal tensions are invented and labeled fiction. The
campaign outline proposes names and arcs for owner review. They don't stand in for
the actual reporters who broke the story. Credit Star reporters Robyn Doolittle and
Kevin Donovan and Gawker's reporting in historical notes without fictionalizing
their private encounters or claiming this game's reporter did their work.

Real public figures appear only through attributed public events and dated summaries.
No invented criminal conspiracy, evidence theft, raids, interview with a secret
dealer, addiction minigame, health diagnosis or private family dialogue. Toronto
neighborhoods are lived-in communities, not shorthand for criminality. Satire can
target publicity, bureaucracy and newsroom deadline chaos while maintaining human
stakes. The story's emotional resolution concerns the fictional reporting team.

Earlier owner-held material was reviewed during the completed asset import #103.
Retain Ford Frenzy, jr42 productions, the recovered visual palette and suitable art
references. Adapt the Crack Nation identity into new title art; recompose City Hall
hallway/office art for search scenes. Retire combat, attacks, damage and action-game
progression. Old budgets/build logs are historical concept evidence, not current
scope, estimates or proof of a finished story. Public conclusions only; private
correspondence and source archive locations are intentionally absent here.
